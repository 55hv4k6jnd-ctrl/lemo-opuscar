# ROPE & STEAM — treatment

Style: **1920s Silent Film** (`styles/silent-film/STYLE.md`, kept whole).
Topic (user): a silent animated western in the Yellowstone country. Everything here is original: no characters, names, scenes or music from any existing series or film.
Language: English intertitles. No voice (silent film). Score: original solo upright piano, written for this film. Length ≈ 2 min 35 s. 1920×1080, 24 fps.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The last drive (before / after).** An old rancher's final cattle drive down the Yellowstone valley; at the end he hands his hat to his granddaughter. Tender, but slow and mostly landscape; the turn is a gesture we can see coming.
2. **The trick roper (comedy chain).** A ranch hand at a rodeo tries ever-bigger lasso tricks, each one going wrong in a chain until a geyser launches him. Funny, but a string of gags with no heart, and a crowd to animate.
3. **The runaway calf (single journey with a lesson). ← chosen.** A girl who can't throw a rope chases a runaway calf from the ranch, through a bison herd, up into a boiling geyser basin, and lands the one throw that matters. One subject (Josie), one goal (bring the calf back), one turn (she stops hurrying), an ending that echoes the opening (the fence post, then the hat). It uses the Yellowstone country itself — river valley, lodgepole pines, bison, steam — as the obstacle course, and it gives the medium its signature: a geyser eruption blooming white through the silver print.

## 2. Logline and arc

On a ranch by the Yellowstone River, a girl too hasty to throw a lasso chases a runaway calf into the geyser country — and when the ground starts to boil, she finally slows her hands.

- **Setup**: Grandpa ropes a fence post in one lazy throw; Josie, in a hurry, ropes herself. "Slow hands."
- **Complication**: while she sulks on the gate, the calf Biscuit walks out behind her. Chase across the valley; a near-miss with a bull bison.
- **Turn**: in the steaming basin the crust cracks under the calf. Josie starts to whirl fast, stops, breathes. Silence. Slow hands. The loop lands.
- **Ending**: she drags the calf clear as the geyser erupts. At sunset she ropes Grandpa's fence post in one slow throw; he tips his hat to her; she tips hers and it falls over her eyes. Iris out.

## 3. Benchmark (grammar only)

- **Buster Keaton's railroad and cattle comedies (1920s)**: the gag in one locked wide; a deadpan face, a body that acts; the wait is the joke. *Not taken*: any gag, costume, character or shot.
- **Lynd Ward / Frederic Remington-era engraved western illustration**: hatching for sagebrush and fur, big skies left pale, horizon low. *Not taken*: any composition.
- **1920s photoplay cue sheets**: music by mood section (Pastorale, Hurry, Misterioso, Agitato, Tenderly), a theme that returns changed. *Not taken*: any published melody.

## 4. Characters & marks

- **Josie** (~12, 5-head child): braids, a too-big wide-brim hat (her silhouette mark), checked shirt, dark trousers, boots. Pale silent-era face, dark lids.
- **Grandpa Amos** (7.5-head adult, tall, a slight stoop): tall-crown hat, white walrus moustache (not a toothbrush), long dark coat, chaps. Deadpan; moves slowly.
- **Biscuit** the calf: small, white face on a dark body, oversized ears, wobbly legs.
- **The bull bison**: a huge dark mass of hatched fur, the one creature bigger than the frame.
- **The one colour**: the **lasso** is yellow-ochre — the only hue in the film, kept through the print. The rope is what the film is about (and a nod to the Yellowstone name).

## 10. Tint plan (print as mood control)

Ranch morning: amber, clean. Chase: harsher, more damage, less sepia. Bison: neutral silver. Basin: cold blue, damage up. Eruption: halation bloom. Home: rose/warm, cleanest print. Yellow rope throughout.

# SWEEP — treatment

Style: **Chinese Ink Wash** (`styles/ink-wash/STYLE.md`, kept whole).
An original story in the spirit of Chinese folk-tale animation: a tiny, overlooked mountain imp. No characters, names, scenes, designs or music from any existing film.
Language: English narration and dialogue (voice: Kokoro offline TTS now; ElevenLabs takes can replace it line for line later — see `SCRIPT_ELEVENLABS.md`). Subtitles: English inscriptions.
Length ≈ 5 min. 1920×1080, 24 fps.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The handscroll of one day (a single long pan).** The whole film is one painting unrolled right to left: the gate in the morning, the storm, the village at dawn. Elegant, but five minutes on one camera move goes flat, and the storm needs a break in the flow.
2. **Two brushes in dialogue (the imp vs. the Great King).** The King's heavy wet strokes against the imp's small dry ones, until the imp's stroke wins. Clever, but it makes the film about the King, and the heart (being seen) gets lost.
3. **Three acts with a rhyme: the Gate, the Storm, the Morning. ← chosen.** A comic routine at the gate (nobody knows his name), a storm that empties the mountain and leaves him with a lost child, and a morning when someone finally asks his name. The opening framing of the gate returns at the end, changed. The style's native move is built into the hero: **his broom is a brush.** Every sweep paints a stroke; at the peak, out of a silence, one great flying-white sweep clears a path down the mountain through the storm. At the end he signs his new name with the broom, and the vermilion seal lands.

## 2. Logline and arc

For three hundred years a tiny imp has swept the gate of a demon king's cave and nobody has asked his name — until a storm empties the mountain and leaves him alone with a lost human child.

- **Setup (Gate)**: the routine; a hundred demons with grand names; the imp has none. He sweeps the moon off the pond at night.
- **Turn (Storm)**: the mountain floods; the King and his demons flee; the imp stays and finds a crying child. Demons are meant to frighten children; he has never frightened anyone. Silence. "So I did the only thing I know." One sweep clears the way.
- **Ending (Morning)**: the village waits with lanterns. "What's your name?" He has none; the child gives him one: *Sweep*. Back at the gate, the King bellows "Broom thing!" — "Sweep, sir. My name is Sweep." Someone at the bottom of the mountain waves.

## 3. Benchmark (grammar only)

- **1960s–80s Chinese ink-painting animation** (Shanghai Animation Film Studio): blank paper as space, things painted into existence, guqin pacing. *Not taken*: any character, composition or melody.
- **Quiet first-person picture-book narration** (the narrator understates; the picture carries the joke or the ache). *Not taken*: any text.

## 4. Characters & the one red

- **The imp (Sweep)**: knee-high, one pale ink-wash body like a turnip, two loaded strokes for arms, a single horn-tuft, two dot eyes. He carries a **broom taller than himself** whose bristles are a brush. The broom's **tassel is the film's one vermilion** — it finds him in every wide shot and rhymes with the seal at the end.
- **The Great King**: a huge wet mass of ink (no clear outline), two burning burnt-ink eyes, horns in thick strokes. Loud, cowardly.
- **The hundred demons**: quick xieyi silhouettes — tusks, many eyes, fangs — marching in a line.
- **The child**: a small pale figure in a few strokes, a straw hat, bare feet.

## 7. Subtitles and titles

Inscriptions (Cormorant Garamond italic, ink colour, no box, a small vermilion square at the start of each line), in each shot's empty paper. Title *Sweep* written in vertical brush strokes with a vermilion seal; the end signature rhymes with it.
